Route: estimation (user states visitors were randomly assigned to A or B; comparison is identified by design; no prior claim; no rival explanations needed).
Goal: decide whether to ship B over A on the signup page.
Estimand: difference in signup rate per visit, B minus A (percentage points), and the ratio B/A, over the two-week window.
Population: visitors to the signup page 2026-07-01..2026-07-14, randomized to A or B. Generalizes to future traffic only if it resembles these two weeks.
Uncertainty method: 95% Wald interval on the pooled difference and on log rate ratio; cross-check with a day-level bootstrap (20000 resamples of days), which allows for day-to-day variation. Level committed before reading: 95%.
Practical threshold: none supplied by the user. Assumed: any lift that pays for switching cost; reported as-is so user can compare.
Stop condition: interval is computed and the two methods agree; the data cannot tighten it further.
Data validity: 14 days x 2 variants = 28 rows, none missing, no duplicates. Visits balanced (A 14089, B 13961; sample-ratio check z=0.76, p=0.44, no sign of assignment problem). Completeness semantics: rows are daily aggregates; zero-signup days do not appear absent (all cells nonzero). Cannot verify that every visit/signup event reached the export (no independent denominator): UNKNOWN, applies equally to both arms under random assignment.
Result: A 583/14089 = 4.14%; B 666/13961 = 4.77%. Diff +0.63 pp (95% CI 0.15 to 1.12; p=0.010). Relative lift +15.3% (RR 1.153, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.29). Day bootstrap agrees: diff 0.14 to 1.12 pp, RR 1.03 to 1.28. B ahead on 9 of 14 days. Week 1 +0.82 pp, week 2 +0.44 pp (not tested for difference; each half is noisy).
Limitations: assignment randomness is taken from the user's statement and not verifiable in the file; only the balanced visit split is consistent with it. Signup rate is per visit, not per unique visitor; repeat visitors would make observations non-independent (day bootstrap partly covers this). Two weeks only: novelty effect possible (week 2 lift smaller, not conclusive). Signups are the only outcome; no downstream quality (activation, retention). No secondary segments in data.
