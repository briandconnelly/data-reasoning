**The new flow made checkout about 35–40 seconds faster, roughly 19–21%.** That is West going from about 187s to about 147s. I'd use "~37 seconds (~20%)" in the Q2 review. Don't use the naive figure of about 50s.

**Why not 50s**
- Comparing West's whole pre-period with its post-period, then subtracting East's change, gives −50s.
- West was already getting faster before launch because of the shipping-step UX cleanup. The West–East gap shrank about 0.5s per day through February and early March. East was flat and never got the cleanup. Part of that 50s is the cleanup, not the new flow.

**How I got 35–40s**
- West fell abruptly at the 3/15 cutover, from about 187s to about 148s overnight, while East stayed near 180s. I compared West with East, which removes anything that hit both regions.
- Compared with the trend the cleanup had already set, the step on day one is −35s. A trend fitted to only the last three pre-weeks gives the same −35s.
- Compared with the level of the last pre-launch week, the post-period is −40s.
- Averaged over all of post-launch against the extrapolated pre-trend, the gap is −32s. I'd set that aside, because it assumes the cleanup would have kept improving indefinitely. West's post-launch slope is flat, so that assumption looks wrong.

**Caveats for the review**
- **The price promotion launched in West on the same day (3/15).** Average order value jumped from about $45 to about $54. I can't separate it from the checkout flow with this data, because both hit West together and East has no promotion. A discount is unlikely to speed up checkout much, but it could change who buys and how fast they complete. Treat the number as "the 3/15 West change", most of which is plausibly the new flow.
- **West was chosen because it had the most complaints.** It started about 25s slower than East, and a region picked for being worst could improve on its own. The sharp step at the cutover argues against that, but it isn't a randomized test.
- **The column is `completion_time_seconds`.** I assumed each row is that day's median and averaged those, so this is an average of daily medians. That's close to the median you asked about, but it isn't the median over all checkouts.
- **I have only about 4 weeks of post-launch data, all flat.** I haven't tested whether the effect lasts. I also haven't put a confidence interval on it.

If you can get the promotion's timing or the checkout-level data, I can tighten this. A test in a region with no promotion would settle it.
